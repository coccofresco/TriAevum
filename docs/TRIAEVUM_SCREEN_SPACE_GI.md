## Conventions and parameters (decisions)

### M1: measure, weights, estimator

- **Measure.** v is the unit vector from the receiver to the camera;
  `SectionFrame::FromView` completes it deterministically (e1 = normalize(h x v)
  with h = X unless |v.x| >= 0.9, then Y; e2 = v x e1). phi in [0, pi), theta in
  [-pi, pi), omega = v cos theta + t(phi) sin theta, d omega = |sin theta| d theta d phi.
- **Weights.** w_j(phi) = (1/pi) integral over the sector of max(0, n.omega) |sin theta|.
  With a = n.v and b = n.t(phi), n.omega = a cos theta + b sin theta. No source
  cosine and no 1/r^2: the receiver cosine is already the solid-angle weight.
- **Own closed forms** (verified against the quadrature, and in M2 against
  [B2], see the report):
  - (the M2 cross-check and the [B2] reintegration compare them with [B2]
    eq. 7, implemented from the paper: agreement to 1.7e-16)
  - per piece where sin theta has one sign s and the lobe is positive:
    integral_p^q (a cos t + b sin t) sin t dt = 1/2 [b (D - sin D cos S) + a sin D sin S],
    D = q - p, S = q + p, evaluated as b ((D - sin D) + 2 sin D sin^2(S/2)) + a sin D sin S
    with a Taylor series for D - sin D on small D (no cancellation in FP32);
  - the lobe is [g - pi/2, g + pi/2], g = atan2(b, a), wrapped into [-pi, pi];
  - section mass M_phi = (r/pi)(cos g' + g' sin g'), r = |(a, b)|,
    g' = min(|g|, pi - |g|). M_phi = 0 only when n is normal to the section plane;
    then every contribution is zero and nothing is divided.
- **Sectors** are uniform in theta over [-pi, pi) (baseline); the count N is
  independent of the section count S. A sector takes the field at its centre
  direction (deterministic centre rule). The continuous reference integrates
  the same section without sectors to measure quantization.
- **Estimator.** uniform p = 1/pi, phi_s = pi (s + u)/S with a declared rotation
  u in [0, 1) (u = 0.5 in the deterministic mode; per-pixel/per-frame u is the
  production randomization, from M3). J is never renormalized by the lit or
  visible fraction; open directions take the declared fallback (zero: no local
  GI, any environment enters separately).
- **Outputs.** J raw (never clamped); A raw (can exceed 1 for few sections:
  a grazing normal gives up to pi/2 for one section) and A = clamp(A raw, 0, 1);
  C = valid / scheduled candidates (unobservable directions count as open for A,
  lower C only: optimistic AO fallback, diagnostic only).
- **Inputs are declared, never inferred.** `InputDeclaration` +
  `ValidateInputDeclaration` reject an unknown depth encoding, forward axis,
  motion direction/units/jitter, radiance profile or encoding, receiver albedo
  encoding, exposure, a geometric normal outside view space, a source that is
  not outgoing radiance, or indistinguishable sky/unavailable pixels.
  `LdrApproximation` is a separate profile and is never calibrated radiance.
- **Radiometry.** `SourceRadianceFromIrradiance` = rho_s E / pi, applied once by
  the adapter; the source albedo is never an input of the transport.
  `ComposeIndirect` = rho_d J, applied once; `ComposeNative` implements
  C_out = L_direct,d + L_spec + L_e + A L_ambient,d + rho_d J_local.

### M2: gathering inputs (`GatherInput`, `ValidateGatherInput`)

- **View space and pixels.** Camera at the origin, forward -Z, view depth
  z = -Z > 0. Viewport texel (i, j) covers [i, i+1) x [j, j+1), centre
  (i + 1/2, j + 1/2); normalized viewport UV = pixel / extent. The viewport is a
  rectangle of the buffers; texels outside it are never read (tested with a
  poisoned border, CPU and GPU).
- **Rays.** X/z = ViewX . (u, v, 1), Y/z = ViewY . (u, v, 1), declared by the
  adapter (the same affine form as our GTAO provider: it carries storage
  orientation and off-centre projection). The 2x2 linear part must be
  invertible.
- **Depth.** `DepthEncoding` with near and far: perspective z = n f / (f - d (f - n));
  reversed z = n f / (n + d (f - n)), or n / d with an infinite far plane
  (only reversed); W-buffer z = n + d (f - n); reversed W-buffer
  z = f - d (f - n). A geometry texel whose decoded depth is non-finite or
  outside [n (1 - 1e-5), f (1 + 1e-5)] becomes unavailable.
- **Classes.** `TexelClass` per texel, declared by the adapter: Unavailable,
  OneSided, TwoSided, Sky. Sky is observed emptiness (valid for C, open);
  Unavailable lowers C. Receivers that are not geometry, or have no geometric
  normal, output the neutral values J = 0, A raw = A = 1, C = 0, Valid = false.
- **Source radiance.** Linear outgoing radiance per texel (3 floats). The
  one-sided test uses the source's geometric normal (below).
- **Geometric normal (section 1, M1 open point 3).** `GeometricNormalSource`
  has two declared sources. `RendererFaceNormal` is the primary one (decision
  of 2026-10-03: the renderer will write a per-pixel face normal of the
  visible surface, from the rasterized triangles, in view space, unit length,
  oriented toward the eye, by the same draws that write scene depth; format
  and channels later); the module normalizes it and orients it toward the
  camera (the visible side is the front side). `DepthReconstruction` is the
  fallback and the test reference. The
  reconstruction scores each 2x2 quadrant (sx, sy) by the 1/z planarity errors
  it can test (on a plane 1/z is affine on screen): the 2x2 term
  |w(d) - w(x) - w(y) + w(p)| and the second differences
  |w(p) - 2 w(x) + w(2x)|, |w(p) - 2 w(y) + w(2y)| along its sides. The best
  quadrant gives both tangents, so an edge texel never mixes two planes.
  Errors within 4e-6 w(p) are ties (FP32 rounding must not decide); ties go
  to the quadrant with more verified second differences, then to the fixed
  priority +x+y, -x+y, +x-y, -x-y; a quadrant without its diagonal ranks after
  every full one. Faces thinner than three texels (grazing views) are
  ambiguous for any 5-tap stencil: declared limit (0.32% of the test texels).

### M2: sections, candidates and segments (`GatherSettings`)

- **Sections on screen.** phi is uniform in the 3D frame around v (M1), so the
  density stays p = 1/pi. The screen direction of +t is the derivative of
  (X/z, Y/z) along P + mu t, g = (t.x z_p + P.x t.z, t.y z_p + P.y t.z),
  mapped through the inverse of the declared UV-to-ray map and normalized in
  pixels.
- **Projected radius per side.** The rays of the section plane at angle
  asin(R / |P|) from the receiver's ray bound the R-ball exactly; their image
  gives the screen extent of each side. If R >= |P| (the ball contains the
  camera) or the bounding ray is not in front of the camera, the extent is
  the viewport diagonal. R is metric (scene units) in every test; nothing
  depends on the pixel resolution.
- **Schedules.** `EveryTexel` (reference): every texel crossed by the line
  within the extent, by exact grid traversal from the receiver centre
  (boundary parameters (k + 1/2)/|d|, no accumulated increments); a texel is
  represented by the line point nearest to its centre, clamped to its chord;
  chords shorter than 1e-3 px (corner touches) are skipped; exact corners
  skip both touching texels. `Radial` (production candidate): K positions per
  side at lambda_k = lambda_0 + (L - lambda_0) ((k + jitter)/K)^exponent,
  lambda_0 the exit of the receiver texel; the jitter covers the contact
  region over frames. A texel is one surface sample: a position falling in the
  same texel as the previous one on that side is skipped (counted).
- **Receiver exclusion.** The receiver texel is never a candidate.
- **Candidate segment (section 4).** q is reconstructed from the continuous UV
  of the line and the pointwise depth of the selected texel, so it stays in
  the section plane (the raster-footprint error remains, as specified). The
  back segment follows the camera ray through q for tau, clipped to the
  R-sphere around the receiver (closed-form quadratic). Exclusions: invalid
  or unavailable texels and texels outside the viewport (scheduled, not
  valid); sky (valid, open); front point beyond R (policy: excluded even if
  its thickness segment would enter the sphere; measured in T09); a segment
  passing within 1e-6 z_P of the receiver (policy: degenerate, rejected).
- **Self-intersection.** A candidate is the receiver's own surface when its
  texel-centre point Q (exact reconstruction, no footprint error) satisfies
  (Q - P) . n <= 2e-3 |Q - P| + 1e-5 z_P, n the receiver's geometric normal.
  Such candidates are rejected; so are points below the tangent plane, which
  have zero weight anyway. The tolerance ignores occluders less than 0.11
  degrees above the tangent plane: at most sin^2(0.11 deg) = 4e-6 of the cosine
  hemisphere.
- **Intervals.** theta(X) = atan2((X - P).t, (X - P).v) at both ends; the
  segment subtends the short arc between them (< pi); an arc through +-pi is
  split. hi <= lo is an empty interval (zero thickness): no occupancy, no NaN.
- **Masks (section 4, 9).** N sectors uniform in theta, N in [1, 256] in up to
  eight uint32 words (no 64-bit integers). Centre rule: sector j is covered
  by [lo, hi) iff lo <= -pi + (j + 1/2) 2 pi / N < hi, computed as the index
  range [ceil(x(lo)), ceil(x(hi))), x(a) = (a + pi) N / (2 pi) - 1/2. Word
  masks never shift by 32 (count 32 is a constant mask). A sector is claimed
  once per section by the first candidate covering its centre, also with zero
  radiance; weights are the M1 closed form evaluated once per contiguous run
  of newly claimed sectors (runs split at word boundaries); A uses the runs
  of open sectors.
- **Continuous variant (reference only).** The exact union of occupied
  intervals; each candidate adds its new pieces with exact weights.
- **Order and tie-break.** Candidates of both sides are merged by screen
  distance; equal distance: + side first. Because the sides are disjoint in
  theta (derivation 1), the tie-break and the interleaving do not change the
  result; they are kept for determinism.
- **Radiance attribution.** Newly claimed sectors take the candidate's
  radiance toward the receiver; a OneSided source whose geometric normal
  faces away from the receiver (n_q . (P - q) <= 0) occupies with zero
  radiance; TwoSided always emits; no source cosine. A source without a
  normal keeps its radiance (counted).
- **Outputs and counters.** J (raw), A raw, A = clamp(A raw), C = valid /
  scheduled (0 when nothing was scheduled), Valid, occupied sectors, and
  counters (scheduled, valid, sky, self-rejected, beyond R, degenerate,
  back-facing, missing source normal, duplicates, occupying).

## Own derivations (M2)

1. **Sides are disjoint in theta.** For any point X on a camera ray on the +
   side of the section line, (X - P).t = X.t - P.t = X.t > 0, because t is
   orthogonal to v (and to P) and the ray direction r has r.t > 0 on that side.
   So + side intervals lie in (0, pi) and - side intervals in (-pi, 0); the
   back segment stays on the same ray. With N even, theta = 0 and +-pi are
   sector boundaries; with N odd the sector centred on theta = 0 (the direction
   toward the camera, never occluded by visible geometry) is never covered.
2. **Screen order is the first hit among the represented segments.** Within a
   section every candidate segment lies on its own camera ray, i.e. at a fixed
   screen distance. Along a fixed direction omega(theta) from P, the image of
   P + rho omega moves monotonically away from the receiver pixel
   (d/d rho of the in-plane projection is z_P sin theta / (z_P - rho cos theta)^2 > 0
   for theta in (0, pi), mirrored on the other side). Hence the segment that
   covers a direction first in screen order is the nearest one along that
   direction. Screen order differs from the true 3D first hit only through
   what the single layer does not represent: hidden surfaces, the thickness
   model and the exclusion of fronts beyond R (T09).
3. **Screen direction and extent** formulas above (derivative of the
   perspective projection; bounding rays of the R-disk in the section plane).
4. **Segment clipping**: |q - P + lambda r|^2 = R^2 with |q - P| <= R has the
   root lambda = -(q - P).r + sqrt(((q - P).r)^2 - (|q - P|^2 - R^2)) >= 0.
5. **Quadrant normal** (above): 1/z affine on a plane; the 2x2 term misses a
   crease along a screen axis when both planes have the same 1/z gradient
   along that axis (found by the one-sided panel test), the second
   differences catch it; a tie with unverified second neighbours goes to the
   verified quadrant (found by the plane test).
6. **Single-flip bound.** A sector of width 2 pi/N has weight <= (1/pi)(2 pi/N) = 2/N,
   so one sector claimed differently in one section changes A by at most
   2 pi/(N S) and J by that times the radiance: the FP32 tolerance model.
7. **Ignored band of the self tolerance**: the cosine-weighted mass within
   elevation beta of the tangent plane is sin^2 beta.

## [B2] cross-check of M1 (new in M2)

[B2] eqs. 3, 5, 7 and 8 integrate cos(theta - gamma)^+ |sin theta| over theta
measured from the view vector, multiply by the norm of the normal projected on
the slice and integrate phi over [0, pi) with 1/pi. With a = n.v = r cos gamma
and b = n.t = r sin gamma this is exactly M1's (1/pi) max(0, a cos + b sin) |sin|:
same measure, same normalization. The test `B2CrossCheckOfM1ClosedForms`
implements eq. 7 from the paper and compares:

| Quantity | Cases | Max abs difference |
|---|---|---|
| Section mass M_phi (horizons at the lobe edges) | 78 normals | 1.1e-16 |
| Per-piece integral between signed horizons h1 <= 0 <= h2 | 702 | 1.7e-16 |
| Cumulative C_phi from the lobe start | 78 normals x 63 angles | 5.6e-16 |

Agreement wherever both measure the same quantity. Differences of scope, not
of value: eq. 7 needs h1 <= 0 <= h2 inside the lobe (horizons), M1 handles any
interval, wrapped lobes and back-facing normals (it extends eq. 7 through
M(gamma) = M(pi - gamma)); and gamma must be signed by the side of the
projected normal (the text defines it with arccos): with |gamma| the example
weight is 0.0404 instead of 0.0253. This cross-check covered eq. 7 only; the
decisions on every other [B2] topic (horizon search, thickness heuristic,
falloff, pixel radius, sampling, multi-bounce, bent normals, specular
occlusion) are now made with the paper in hand in the next section.

## [B2] reintegration (2026-10-03)

M1 (ccda246) was built without [B2] (its download failed twice) and M2
(c45e89b) checked only eq. 7. This review read the whole paper (9 pages,
figures 1-14, eqs. 1-26, appendices A and B) from
`B2-PracticalRealtimeStrategiesTRfinal.pdf`, equations from the page images
and from 220 dpi renders of the equation regions, and maps every topic to the
specification, to M1/M2 and to a decision. Only the paper was used: no code,
shader or asset associated with it, and not the repository's own GTAO
provider or its GPU test (not opened for this review). The new tests are in
`runtime/three_ds_recomp/tests/renderer_diffuse_gi_b2_tests.cpp` (4 tests in
`renderer_diffuse_gi_reference_tests`); they implement [B2]'s equations with
their own quadrature, depth decoding, section setup and texel enumeration
and compare them with M1's integrator and M2's gather. No M1/M2 code changed.

### Own derivations ([B2])

1. **Eq. 7 as an antiderivative.** cos(t - gamma) sin t = (sin(2t - gamma) + sin gamma)/2,
   so F(h) = 1/4 (-cos(2h - gamma) + cos gamma + 2 h sin gamma) = integral_0^h cos(t - gamma) sin t dt.
   Eq. 7 is F(h1) + F(h2) for h1 <= 0 <= h2; [p, q] on one side is F(q) - F(p)
   (p >= 0) or F(p) - F(q) (q <= 0). Valid inside the lobe only: horizons are
   clamped to gamma +- pi/2 first (eq. 5 has the positive part).
2. **Eq. 6 as written.** With ^+ every horizon is at most pi/2 from v. Per
   section the open lobe then misses its part beyond pi/2 on the side the
   normal leans to ([pi/2, gamma + pi/2] for gamma > 0, mirrored for gamma < 0),
   whose weight F(gamma + pi/2) - F(pi/2) is (r/2) gamma sin gamma: half of the
   gamma term of pi M_phi = r (cos gamma + gamma sin gamma). For a normal tilted alpha from
   v, (1/pi) integral r cos gamma d phi = (1/pi) integral n.v d phi = cos alpha and the
   whole integral is 1, so (1/pi) integral r gamma sin gamma d phi = 1 - cos alpha and an
   open receiver gets A = cos alpha + (1 - cos alpha)/2 = (1 + cos alpha)/2.
3. **Infinite thickness is the height field, up to a computable gap.** With
   tau = infinity a candidate occupies [theta_front, theta_back(R)] on its camera
   ray; theta_front is never above its side's horizon (the minimum front
   angle), and the sides are disjoint (M2 derivation 1). So the gather's
   occluded set is a subset of [B2]'s ([horizon, lobe edge] per side) and
   A_gather = A_B2 + Gap, Gap = weight of [horizon, edge] minus the union of
   the segments. With R unbounded a segment reaches pi - psi(q) (psi the camera
   angle between the receiver and q), and Gap is zero unless the height field
   is open below the horizon inside the section plane.
4. **Raster convergence of eq. 6 sampling.** [B2]'s unit pixel steps and the
   specification's every-crossed-texel rule put their points on the same line,
   within the same texel chords except for texels the steps skip, so their
   horizons differ by at most one texel footprint seen from the receiver:
   first order in the pixel size.

## GPU compute twin (`diffuse_gi_gpu_tests`)

Three GLSL 450 kernels compiled at run time by shaderc: the normal pass (one
invocation per viewport texel), the gather (one per viewport pixel; two
grid-traversal or radial cursors merged by screen distance, eight mask words,
per-run weights) and a unit kernel (masks and weights). Storage buffers:
depth, class, radiance, normals (in/out), results (J, A raw), aux (C, valid,
scheduled, occupied); parameters in a 128-byte std140 block. Explicit
barrier between the normal pass (producer) and the gather (consumer). No
barriers inside the kernels, so out-of-range invocations return at once;
extents are not multiples of the 8 x 8 tile. Guard sentinels after the output
arrays and a poisoned buffer border around a shifted viewport detect
out-of-range writes and reads.

**FP32 tolerance model.** Continuous quantities agree to rounding (mean
|dJ|/max(1, J) and mean |dA| < 2e-5). Discrete decisions (sector centre, chord,
self-intersection and R thresholds) may flip in isolated pixels (< 1%), each
flip bounded by 2 pi/(N S) in A and that times the radiance in J (derivation 6),
at most two flips per pixel. A texel on two planes may take either plane's
normal within the tie tolerance (<= 0.1% of texels, never a mixed normal).
Masks are bit-exact against the FP32 CPU rule; sector weights within 2e-6.

**Baked lighting (compatibility path, section 8).** OoT3D bakes fill and
ambient light into vertex colours and textures; native fragment ambient is
almost zero in the forest. The native colour cannot be split exactly into
albedo, direct and baked light. The declared compatibility path is therefore:
source = the per-pixel *direct* radiance only (never the native colour, never
ambient or fill), receiver albedo = TEV with unit lighting, and
`C_out = native colour + rho_d J` (additive local bounce, A optionally on the
native ambient-like part only through an explicit, labelled artistic control).
It exists only in Per-pixel and Per-pixel + PBR lighting; Native, Authentic and
F2 keep GI off. Any reduction of existing light is exposed as an artistic
control, never hidden in the estimator.

## M3: accumulation and filter (T11-T16)

Implemented 2026-10-03 from the specification (sections 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12),
[B3] and [B6] (papers only) and [B2] section 4.1 (rotations). No temporal
filter, motion-vector, TAA or denoiser implementation was read: not the
repository's own, not FidelityFX, NRD or any `_deps` code, not the GLSL
listing at the end of [B6].

### Pipeline (per view and frame)

1. **Gather** (M2) with one section per pixel and a per-pixel rotation from
   the stratified sequence (below).
2. **Stratum reconstruction** of the raw J and A over a geometry-gated
   window of radius T/2 with half weights at its ends (5 x 5 for T = 4).
   Every residue modulo T, hence every stratum of the tile, has total weight
   1 in a flat region and the window is symmetric (no half-pixel shift).
   Neighbours are gated by the hard surface test of the reprojection (below).
   It acts on the fresh samples only, so blur never accumulates.
3. **Temporal accumulation** (this is the history, saved before the a-trous
   filter): reprojection through the declared motion, 2 x 2 bilinear taps
   each tested on its own; rejected taps' weight is redistributed over the
   accepted ones ([B3] section 4.1). A full rejection is the neutral state
   (alpha = 1). J and its luminance second moment use
   alpha = max(alpha_min, 1 / (N + 1), light response); A has its own history,
   length and light response. N = 1 / alpha (effective sample count).
4. **A-trous filter**, 4 levels with steps 1, 2, 4, 8, B3-spline kernel,
   hard surface gate, colour weight driven by the variance of the estimates,
   variance propagated per level ([B3] section 4.3). Its output is never fed
   back.

Outputs per pixel: accumulated (pre-filter) J and A raw, their variances,
filtered J, filtered A raw and A clamped to [0, 1], alpha, N, light-response
z, history acceptance, motion class and confidence (diagnostics: J raw, J
filtered, A, C from M2, history rejection).

### Conventions and decisions

- **Rotation sequence** (`RotationSequence`, own construction). T x T tile
  (T = 2^L), cycle F, M = T^2 F strata, u = (k + 1/2) / M, k = F c + t:
  fine stratum t = frame mod F; coarse stratum c = ((b XOR e) + s) mod T^2
  with b the recursive ordered-dither index of the tile position (bit pairs
  (0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1) -> 0, 2, 3, 1, lowest pair weighted most), e =
  frame mod 2 and s = 2 bitrev((frame mod T^2) / 2) over log2(T^2 / 2) bits.
  Any F consecutive frames of a tile cover all M strata exactly once (the
  rotations of the deterministic S = M estimator); over T^2 frames every
  stratum visits every tile position. Tiles are anchored at the viewport
  origin. A second, lattice dimension frac(k P / M) (P coprime to M nearest
  0.618 M) gives the per-pixel radial jitter of the Radial schedule. Fixed
  mode is the deterministic, noise-free mode of M1/M2 (bit-identical).
- **Motion.** previous UV = current UV + motion (`CurrentToPrevious`,
  normalized viewport UV); `PreviousToCurrent` and pixel units are converted
  by the declared sign and scale. Jitter in pixels, positive along +u and +v
  (jittered projection = unjittered + jitter). Motion that includes jitter
  is used as is; motion that excludes it gets the jitter difference
  (previous - current) once.
- **Validation** (`HistoryValidation`). `PreviousPosition`: the expected
  previous surface point P' is the adapter's previous position. `FlowAndDepth`
  (declared weaker): P' = CurrentToPrevious(P), the static prediction; where
  its projection differs from the flow by more than 0.25 px the surface is
  moving and only a weak test applies (previous depth within 5% of the
  prediction, normals); such histories are capped at 0.25 / alpha_min frames
  (confidence 0.25). Tap test (both modes): |n_h . (X_h - P')| <=
  0.05 |X_h - P'| + 1e-3 z', n_h . R n >= 0.9, equal surface ids when both are
  known, finite values, inside the previous viewport. z is never compared
  with z: the current surface is moved into the previous camera.
- **Resets.** First frame, camera cut, view change (each view id needs its
  own `TemporalAccumulator`; stereo views have independent histories),
  resize or dynamic resolution (decision: reset, no resampling), explicit
  reset or resource invalidation (`Invalidate()`), transport settings (any
  change of `TransportKey`: R, tau, S, N, schedule), an invalid exposure
  ratio, and temporal off. Projection changes are handled by the previous
  ray map. A reset never reads the previous history (GPU test: NaN-poisoned
  history), and every history texel is written every frame (neutral values
  for non-receivers, borders and sky).
- **Pre-exposure.** J inputs are pre-exposed; on a change the history and
  its second moment are rescaled by r and r^2 (default) or invalidated.
- **Variance.** Second moment of luminance m2 (m1 is the luminance of the
  history, same alpha). Per-sample variance: m2 - m1^2 from N >= 4 ([B3]
  section 4.2), otherwise the spatial variance of the reconstruction window
  (weighted, divided by (sum w)^2 / sum w^2). Variance of the estimate:
  sigma^2 v with v the sum of squared sample weights, v' = (1 - alpha)^2 v + alpha^2.
- **Light response** (own): z = |l(x) - l(H)| / sqrt(sigma^2 (1 + v) + (0.02 max(l(x), l(H)))^2)
  raises alpha linearly from z = 3 to z = 6; A uses an absolute floor 0.01. No
  neighbourhood clamping of the history (optional in the specification;
  not adopted, it can hide motion errors).
- **Filter** (`FilterSettings`). a_pq = h(q) g(p, q) w(p, q), h = (1, 4, 6, 4, 1) / 16
  per axis with holes 2^i ([B6] eqs. 1-4, [B3] eq. 1); g the hard surface
  gate; w = exp(-d / (sigma_l sqrt(Var_p + Var_q) + 0.01 (l_p + l_q) / 2)) with
  d = 0.2126 |dR| + 0.7152 |dG| + 0.0722 |dB| (an equal-luminance chromatic
  edge still stops the filter; scale invariant: J x k, Var x k^2 give J' x k);
  A has its own w. Invalid coordinates are discarded before any read; null
  normals, invalid depth, sky and non-finite values are not receivers
  (neutral J = 0, A = 1, variance 0) and never contribute.
- **Precision and workgroups.** History, moments and weights in FP32
  (64 bytes per pixel, four vec4); 8 x 8 workgroups, no barriers inside the
  kernels; one dispatch per a-trous level with an explicit barrier between
  producer and consumer.

### Own derivations (M3)

1. **Pairwise cancellation.** In the half-weight window the mean offset of
   residue d (mod T) relative to the receiver is d for d < T/2, 0 for d = T/2
   (split) and d - T above. For T = 4 the residues d and d + 2 have mean offsets
   summing to zero for every d, in x and in y; b XOR 1 moves every stratum by
   (T/2, T/2). Hence two consecutive frames cancel the first-order term
   sum_k grad J(u_k) . (q_k - p) by which neighbours' strata would leave a
   tile-periodic error (T = 2 too; not for T = 8).
2. **Full-period cancellation.** If every stratum visits every tile position
   once, the period average of the reconstruction is sum_r w_r J_full(p + o_r):
   a translation-invariant blur of the per-pixel full estimates, with no
   tile-periodic term at any order.
3. **Coprime cycle.** Frame t gives coarse stratum c to position r with fine
   stratum t mod F; the frames that give c to r recur with period T^2, so they
   meet only fine strata congruent modulo gcd(T^2, F). F coprime with T^2 is
   required for every (position, coarse stratum) pair to meet all fine strata.
4. **The reconstruction blur has zeros at the tile frequencies.** The window
   is a 4-pixel box per axis; its transfer function vanishes at 1/4, 1/2, 3/4
   cycles per pixel, so (estimate - S) keeps the true signal's detail at
   exactly the tile bins. A sampling pattern must be measured against K * S
   (the reference through the same window); (K * S - S) is the reconstruction's
   blur bias, reported separately.
5. **Variance of the accumulated estimate:** H = sum_i w_i x_i with
   independent x_i of variance sigma^2 has variance sigma^2 sum w_i^2; with
   H' = (1 - alpha) H + alpha x, v' = (1 - alpha)^2 v + alpha^2. The difference
   x - H has variance sigma^2 (1 + v) (light-response statistic).
6. **Jitter compensation** (motion without jitter): a point at current
   jittered centre c has unjittered position c - j_t, so its previous jittered
   position is c + m + (j_(t-1) - j_t).
7. **Exposure:** J and m1 scale by r = e_t / e_(t-1), m2 by r^2, v and A not.
8. **Colour distance:** sum_c k_c |dJ_c| >= |sum_c k_c dJ_c| = |d l|, equal when
   the channels change in the same direction; positive for equal-luminance
   chromatic edges.

### Deviations from the M3 plan

- The cycle is 7, not 6 (derivation 3), and the coarse shift has its own

- The [B2] 4 x 4 reconstruction is a stratum reconstruction applied to the

- Requirement 1 is measured against K * S (derivation 4): against S alone a

- A light response replaces neighbourhood clamping.
- No sector-grid offset: the M2 sector study already found the quantization

- Separate host target `renderer_diffuse_gi_temporal_tests`; the M2 gather

### Design and conventions

- **Hierarchy** (`DepthHierarchy`, section 5). Level l cell (I, J) covers the
  aligned 2^l x 2^l footprint clipped to the viewport. `ZMin`/`ZMax` bound the
  geometry depths (limits, never a surface). `kCellGeometry`: every texel is
  geometry with a geometric normal; `kCellSky`/`kCellUnavailable`: what else
  it holds. Discontinuity mask: `kCellPlanar` only when the footprint is one
  surface: all geometry of one class, and the planes of the four children
  agree with the cell's plane at the four corners of each child's footprint
  within `PlaneTolerance` (relative inverse depth) and their normals within
  `NormalCos`. Planes: inverse depth w = 1/z is affine in pixels on a plane;
  level 0 takes it from the texel's depth and geometric normal,
  w(x, y) = (n . ray(x, y)) / (n . P) (gradients (n_x a_x + n_y a_y) / (W n.P), ...);
  a planar cell stores the mean of its children's planes at its centre (same
  basis, level-0 pixels). A texel with non-finite radiance is never planar.
- **Footprint level** of a radial sample: the largest l <= `HierarchyMaxLevel`
  with 2^l <= `HierarchySpacingScale` x (spacing to the next sample along the
  line, pixels of this image). Descent: while the level-l cell holding the
  sample's texel is not planar, or holds the receiver's texel, l decreases;
  level 0 is the M2 point reference.
- **Footprint strips** (`HierarchyFootprint`, own construction). A sample on a
  planar cell above level 0 represents the cell's footprint along the line:
  the chord [la, lb] of the section line through the cell is lifted onto the
  cell's plane (exact surface points, no raster footprint error), clipped to
  the R-ball (the M2 front policy applied along the strip), and each end gets
  its thickness segment along its camera ray, clipped to the R-sphere as in
  M2. The strip occupies the shortest arc holding its four corner angles; an
  arc of pi or more, or an edge passing within 1e-6 z_P of the receiver, is
  degenerate. A cell on the receiver's own plane is the receiver's surface
  (self). One cell is one surface sample (duplicates by cell). The one-sided
  test uses the cell's normal (one sign for the whole plane). Depth and
  radiance of a strip always describe the same planar surface; a coarse level
  never joins a near dark object with a far bright background (the mask).
- **Radiance cache** (`CacheMode::Mean`): the mean source radiance of a planar
  footprint, every texel weighted 1 (the screen-uniform density of the
  reference schedule), rebuilt each frame from the source buffer (a
  reconstruction of the source signal, not a bounce). Without the footprint,
  the cache changes only the radiance of point samples (duplicates stay by
  texel).
- **SH backend** (`CacheMode::SphericalHarmonics`). Real orthonormal SH, view
  space, zenith +Z, [B5] eq. 3 convention (Y_1,1 ~ x, Y_1,0 ~ z, Y_1,-1 ~ y; no
  Condon-Shortley phase), index l^2 + l + m, order 2 (9 coefficients), separate
  RGB, no chroma compression. Represented function, for omega from the source
  to the receiver: N(omega) = sum_i L_i max(0, n_i . omega), D(omega) = sum_i max(0, n_i . omega);
  outgoing radiance = N / D (radiance, not cosine-convolved irradiance).
  Operator: N_lm += L_i A_l Y_lm(n_i), D_lm += A_l Y_lm(n_i) with A_l of [B5]
  eqs. 6 and 8 (Funk-Hecke); coefficients are sums in one basis, never clipped.
  The side test stays the gather's one-sided test; the SH is read only on
  planar cells.
- **Metric radial distribution** (`CandidateSchedule::MetricRadial`, section 6).
  Sample k of K lies on the camera ray of the section plane whose closest
  distance to the receiver is rho_k = R ((k + j) / K)^e (the ray at angle
  asin(rho / |P|) from the receiver's ray; rho = R is the exact bound of the
  R-ball, the M2 extent). Distribution function: the fraction of samples within
  closest distance rho is (rho / R)^(1/e); effective metric radius
  R ((K - 1 + j) / K)^e, R as j -> 1. Positions closer than the contact sample
  (the midpoint of the line's chord through the first texel after the
  receiver's, the first EveryTexel texel) are moved onto it: every jitter
  j < j_c samples the contact texel, so no jitter skips it systematically. All
  quantities are metric or exact projections: the schedule is invariant to
  scaling scene, R and tau.
- **Guiding** (section 6, p(phi) = (1 - eta)/pi + eta g(phi)). Regions of
  2^RegionLog2 pixels, B equal bins over [0, pi). Bin probabilities
  q_b = (1 - eta)/B + eta E_b / sum E; cumulative P_(b+1) = P_b + q_b with P_B = 1
  (no rounded renormalization). Draw phi = (b + (u - P_b)/(P_(b+1) - P_b)) pi/B
  for u = (s + rotation)/S (the M1-M3 rotations, so stratification is kept in
  the cumulative domain); its density p_b = (P_(b+1) - P_b) B/pi weights J. The
  table is frozen while a frame samples; `Update()` reads one record per pixel
  and section (luminance of F, p_b, bin) and writes the next state into
  separate storage: e_b = (1/n) sum_(in b) lum(F)/p_b, E_b <- (1 - beta) E_b + beta e_b
  (E = e on first data). Uniform on initialization, `Invalidate()` (scene
  change), zero sums, negative or non-finite records (per region); a region
  without receivers keeps its memory. A: `IndependentUniform` (default)
  evaluates S extra sections at the unguided angles, so A is exactly the
  unguided A; `SamePdf` weights the open weights by the same 1/p_b. No ratio
  estimator, adaptive normalization or clamp is used (the mixture bounds
  1/p_b by pi/(1 - eta)). The guide is a distribution over phi, not a history
  of light.
- **Noise** (section 6, 12). `GenerateBlueNoiseMask`: void and cluster ([B4]
  section 3.1: initial pattern from a seeded SplitMix64, tightest-cluster /
  largest-void swaps until they coincide, then the three ranking phases) with
  the spatiotemporal energy of [B4] eq. 4 (Gaussian over toroidal distance
  within a slice or along a pixel's time column, zero otherwise). Ranks are a
  permutation of [0, W H D); u = (rank + 1/2)/(W H D) at (x mod W, y mod H,
  frame mod D); the radial jitter reads the same slice at (x + W/2, y + H/2)
  ([B4] section 5.2). Deterministic noise-free modes are kept (Fixed, M3
  Stratified). Asset format: "DGIBNM01", extents, flags, sigma, density, seed,
  uint32 ranks, FNV-1a 64; the parser rejects wrong magic, sizes, extents,
  checksums and non-permutations before any use.

### Own derivations (M4)

1. **Plane form.** For X = z ray(x, y) on the plane n . X = n . P,
   w = 1/z = n . ray(x, y)/(n . P), affine in (x, y) because ray is affine in
   the viewport UV. Two affine functions differ most at the corners of a
   rectangle, so agreement at the four corners bounds the deviation over the
   whole child footprint.
2. **Strip angle.** The front points of a planar strip lie on the line
   plane x section plane; the angle seen from P along a line not through P is
   monotone, so the front contributes its two ends; with the two thickness
   segments the strip's angular set is the arc between its extreme corners
   (declared assumption for the back curve: open point 19).
3. **Unbiased guided estimator.** With u uniform, phi = P^-1(u) has density
   p = dP/dphi = (P_(b+1) - P_b) B/pi in bin b, and E[F(phi)/p(phi)] = integral F dphi;
   stratified u keeps each stratum unbiased. Using the stored density of the
   draw (derived from the same cumulative) makes draw and weight identical.
4. **Exact edges.** P_b as running sums of q_b = (1 - eta)/B + eta E_b/sum E
   (no division by a rounded total) makes probabilities exact in binary
   (e.g. (1 - eta)/B = 1/32) give identical edges in double, float and GLSL:
   a deterministic u = (s + 1/2)/S that hits an edge selects the same bin in
   every twin (found by the FP32 test: before this, u = 1/8 met an edge of
   1/16 steps and the twins took different bins).
5. **Independent A.** The A sections use phi = pi (s + u)/S, the unguided
   expression, and the same code path: A of a guided frame equals the
   unguided A bit for bit.
6. **SH on one plane.** With one normal, N = L D for any truncation order, so
   N/D = L wherever D > 0; on a planar cell with texture the deviation is
   (radiance contrast) x (spread of the truncated clamped cosines of the
   texels' normals), bounded by the planarity tolerance.
7. **Footprint level under rescaling.** Rendering the same rays at 3x the
   resolution (or zooming the tangents by 3) multiplies every metric sample's
   screen distance and spacing by exactly 3, so the requested level rises by
   floor(log2(3 s)) - floor(log2 s) in {1, 2} and the metric footprint
   2^l x pixel changes by 2/3 or 4/3.

### Deviations from the specification and the plan

- The SH backend exists and is tested but is used only on planar cells, where
  it equals the mean cache; its crease errors exceed any useful tolerance, so
  the specification's rule "reduce the footprint or use point evaluation"
  leaves it without a benefit here (off by default).
- Guiding is implemented and unbiased but off by default: at equal section
  work more stratified uniform sections are better on the synthetic scenes;
  A uses the specification's independent uniform estimator, which doubles the
  section work when guiding is on.
- Blue-noise rotations are off by default: the M3 stratified sequence
  converges better from 8 frames on (the masks help only in the first 2-4
  frames, e.g. after a disocclusion).
- The hierarchy is applied to radial schedules only (EveryTexel remains the
  reference); its samples are strips, an own extension of the "sample
  footprint" of section 5.
- T10 uses the M2 radial schedule as the baseline (the production candidate)
  and EveryTexel as the reference, since every M4 optimization acts on sparse
  schedules.
- The M4 GPU test uses the validation layer without the synchronization
  validation features (one submission per stage).

### Pass interface

`Fast::Renderer::DiffuseGi::DiffuseGiPass`, one instance per view and composed
surface (the history belongs to the instance; stereo views and several
surfaces use several instances).

| Call | Contract |
|---|---|
| `Initialize(PassDevice, CachedPassShaderCompiler&)` | Creates the five set layouts, five pipeline layouts and eight compute pipelines. Each kernel is resolved by its exact source text through the pass shader compiler, like every built-in pass (a pack hit, no compilation). A second overload takes a resolver (tests). Unavailable with a reason when the device binds fewer than 11 storage buffers per stage or cannot store RGBA16F/R32F images |
| `ReplacesResources(...)`, `Configure(extent, settings, inputs, outputs)` | Validates extent, settings and the two output images (format, buffer extent), checks every storage binding against `maxStorageBufferRange` and the dispatch limits, (re)allocates only buffers whose size changes and rewrites descriptor sets only when buffers or outputs change. Settings that size nothing (R, tau, S, N, temporal and filter parameters, filter levels 1-8) change no Vulkan object; `ReplacesResources` tells the caller when its recorded work must be completed first |
| `Buffer(PassResourceId)` | The adapter's input buffers (and, for diagnostics and tests, every other buffer) |
| `Execute(command, PassFrame, J state, A state, completion, record)` | Plans the frame on the CPU (nothing is recorded if a declaration is invalid), then records one parameter update and 14 dispatches (defaults) with 15 private barriers, the two output transitions to `GENERAL` and the two completion transitions the caller's graph asks for |
| `ClearOutputs(...)` | Writes J = 0, receiver 0, A = 1 over the whole buffer extent for a frame without GI and invalidates the history (a gap breaks the one-frame motion chain) |
| `ResetHistory()` | The next frame resets (explicit) |
| `InvalidateScreenResources()`, `Shutdown()` | Release resources (after the caller completed its recorded work); the history is invalidated |
| `Available()`, `UnavailableReason()`, `Stats()`, `Resources()`, `ParameterLayout()`, `LastFrame()`, `LatestHistory()` | Availability with a reason, statistics (configurations, reallocations, executions, clears, failures, reset frames by reason, invalidations, buffer and memory bytes, kernels), the resource plan, the constants of the last frame |

`PassFrame` carries the frame state of section 1: depth camera (encoding,
near, far, UV-to-ray map with jitter), normal source (renderer face normal or
depth reconstruction), frame index (rotation sequence), view id, camera cut,
explicit reset, pre-exposure, jitter, camera transform to the previous view,
motion declaration, history validation and whether surface ids were written.
The transport key is computed by the pass from the settings and the normal
source, so a transport change resets by itself.

Inputs are written by the adapter into the pass-owned buffers with compute
shader or transfer writes recorded earlier on the same queue; the pass's
leading barrier makes them visible. Outputs are the caller's images (the graph
arena in Stage B). The caller owns their Vulkan state (`PassImageState`, its
graph state tracker): the pass transitions from it to `GENERAL` for the
dispatch and to the completion state the graph declares; a write-only
completion (no reader) elides the completion transition. The barrier record
counts planned, emitted and elided output transitions, private barriers and
dispatches.

| Dispatch | Kernel (pack name) | Set | Extent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | normal (`diffuse_gi_normal.comp`) | gather family, M4 block of level 0 | viewport |
| 2-6 | hierarchy level 0, reduce 1-4 (`diffuse_gi_hierarchy_level0.comp`, `diffuse_gi_hierarchy_reduce.comp`) | gather family, M4 block of the level (dynamic offset) | cells of the level |
| 7 | gather (`diffuse_gi_gather.comp`, the M4 kernel) | gather family | viewport |
| 8 | geometry pack (`diffuse_gi_pack.comp`) | pack | viewport |
| 9 | temporal (`diffuse_gi_temporal.comp`) | temporal, history k in, 1 - k out | viewport |
| 10-13 | a-trous levels 0-3 (`diffuse_gi_filter.comp`) | filter: colour -> ping, ping -> pong, pong -> ping (dynamic offset: level block) | viewport |
| 14 | output (`diffuse_gi_output.comp`) | output, colour source = last level (dynamic offset: final or neutral block) | buffer |

Before dispatch 1: a barrier (adapter and earlier frame writes -> reads,
earlier uniform reads -> update), `vkCmdUpdateBuffer` of the parameter buffer,

compute read/write. Without the footprint and the cache the hierarchy is not
dispatched (9 dispatches); with the filter off the temporal colour goes to the
output kernel (5 dispatches with both off). Unused kernel bindings (SH, guide
table, records, noise, absent optional inputs) take a 16-byte placeholder that
no dispatch reads.

### Parameter blocks

One uniform buffer per instance, written once per frame by
`vkCmdUpdateBuffer` (so frames in flight never read a later frame's
constants; at most 65,536 bytes). Blocks start at multiples of
max(16, `minUniformBufferOffsetAlignment`); offsets for alignment 256:

| Offset | Block | Bytes | Bound as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `GatherShaderParams` (M2/M3, `Rotations.Frame` = frame index) | 176 | binding 0 of the gather family and of the pack |
| 256 + 256 l, l = 0-7 | `M4ShaderParams` with `Build.x` = l | 240 | binding 7 of the gather family, dynamic offset |
| 2304 | `TemporalShaderParams` (from `MakeTemporalShaderParams`) | 224 | binding 0 of the temporal sets |
| 2560 + 256 l, l = 0-7 | `FilterShaderParams` with step 2^l | 80 | binding 0 of the filter sets, dynamic offset |
| 4608 | `OutputShaderParams`, final mode | 48 | binding 0 of the output sets, dynamic offset |
| 4864 | `OutputShaderParams`, neutral mode | 48 | idem (`ClearOutputs` updates only this block) |

`OutputShaderParams` (own, std140): `Extent` (buffer width, height, viewport
x, y), `Viewport` (viewport width, height, mode 0 final / 1 neutral, 0),
`Limits` (65504, the largest finite FP16 value, then zeros). Every other block
is built by the M2-M4 function its FP32 twin reads.

### Output kernel (own)

Over the buffer extent: inside the viewport and in final mode, a receiver
(geometry with a finite, non-zero normal and positive view depth) stores
J.rgb, each channel bounded to +-65504 and a non-finite channel stored as 0,
alpha 1, and A = clamp(A raw, 0, 1) (a non-finite A stored as 1); every other
texel stores J = 0, alpha 0, A = 1. The bound is the FP16 storage range, not an
artistic clamp: J stays FP32 through the pipeline and is reduced to FP16 only
here (section 9). A is R32_SFLOAT because it is a core storage format (no
`shaderStorageImageExtendedFormats`) and the renderer's AO resource has the
same format.

### Reset rules in the pass

The pass applies `TemporalHistoryState` (the single owner shared with
`TemporalAccumulator`): first frame, camera cut, view change, resize, explicit
reset or resource invalidation, transport key, exposure, temporal off. Pass
decisions: replacing the history buffers (a resize, or a Configure after
`InvalidateScreenResources`) invalidates a committed history, so a resize
reports resize and explicit; the first allocation reports only the first
frame; `ClearOutputs` invalidates the history; settings that size nothing keep
the buffers and the history (a transport change resets through its key).

### Decisions and deviations

- **Raw Vulkan, title-neutral.** The pass follows the shared-layer pattern
  (`renderer3ds/pica_surface_lighting_pass`: `VkDevice`, `VkCommandBuffer`,
  the pass shader compiler, an optional pipeline cache), not the NRI interop
  of the `fast/extension` passes, so it stays in the generic renderer layer; the
  Stage B provider maps the graph's barrier plan and state tracker onto
  `PassImageState`/`PassOutputAccess` and records into the backend's command
  buffer.
- **Inputs in the module's adapter layout.** The pass owns buffers in the
  layout the M2/M3 kernels declare; the adapter fills them. The kernels stay
  the twins' kernels unchanged (bit-compatible with every M2-M4 test), and the
  title's conventions stay in one adapter kernel (open point 25).
- **Outputs caller-owned**, as the graph arena owns `DiffuseIllumination`; J
  in RGBA16F with the receiver flag in alpha, A in R32F.
- **Parameters through the command stream** (`vkCmdUpdateBuffer`) and dynamic
  uniform offsets for the per-level blocks: no per-frame-slot uniform rings and
  no hazard with frames in flight; the M4 GPU test's one-submission-per-level
  scheme was a test convenience.
- **Guiding, SH and blue noise are not part of the runtime pass**: the M4
  decisions keep them off; `ValidatePassSettings` rejects them, the guide
  update kernel is not in the pack, and their re-measurement on captures
  (open point 20) uses the CPU and GPU twins.
- **Resize resets** with resize and explicit (replaced buffers); no
  resampling (M3 decision). `ClearOutputs` resets the next frame.
- **Not bit-identical to the runtime-compiled twins** (declared tolerance
  above); the stage comparisons use the M2-M4 tolerance models.
- **No aliasing of frame transients** and no work scale in Stage A (open
  point 24); both ping-pong filter buffers exist whenever a level runs, so the
  level count changes without reallocation.
- **At most 64 sections per pixel and frame** in the pass (one dispatch must
  stay short); the M2 studies used up to 64.

### Graph, inputs and resources

- Display plan: input `GlobalIlluminationScreenSpace`; the
  `GlobalIllumination` pass then reads exactly NativeSceneView,
  DiffuseAlbedoGuide, NativeDepth, OutlineGeometryGuide (face normal) and
  MotionVectors and writes DiffuseIllumination; the Guides pass declares the
  geometry guide for it; no specular output, no reflection source.
- PICA extension graph: request `ScreenSpaceGlobalIllumination` (appended
  to `PicaAttachmentFeatureRequests`): the albedo guide and the face normal

  instrumentation publishes the face normal through

- Provider (`fast/extension/screen_space_gi_provider.*`): one pass instance per
  composed surface (namespace, colour and depth addresses; at most three, the
  least recently used retired after the recorded work completed), so every
  surface has its own history; a second composition of a surface in the same
  frame republishes that frame's output without advancing history. The
  DiffuseIllumination output is the effect graph's arena image: its
  dispatch and completion transitions follow the graph's barrier plan and
  state tracker (NRI); the pass records only its private barriers. Histories
  restart on a new world, on a source switched off or returning (the same rule
  as the traced GI) and on screen-resource invalidation (arena
  reconfiguration, render-target reset). A frame without motion vectors or a
  temporal view gets the neutral output (J = 0).
- Adapter (`fast/extension/diffuse_gi_adapter.*`, open point 25): one compute
  kernel per guide texel decodes the declared conventions into the pass
  inputs at the work extent and writes the upsampler's output guide (view
  depth, normal code) at the guide extent; the native depth is sampled
  through the interop's depth-aspect view.
- GPU profiler: the existing `GlobalIllumination` scope covers adapter, pass
  and upsampler (`global_illumination_ms`); per-kernel timing is Stage C.
- Diagnostics (stderr): `TRIAEVUM_SCREEN_SPACE_GI ready=... reason=...`,

  stats line on change (surface, factor, instances, buffer bytes, executions,
  republications, clears, reconfigurations, history resets, last reset
  reasons, allocation time); a fallback line every 300 neutral frames.

### Composition (J once, A not composed)

The scene composite adds `coverage * AO visibility * intensity *
srgb_to_linear(albedo) * DiffuseIllumination.rgb`, with no division by pi:
DiffuseIllumination is J = E / pi before the receiver albedo, so the pass
writes J directly and the albedo multiplies it exactly once in the
composite. The intensity is `DiffuseIntensity * source gain` (the guide
carries the source weights relative to the largest gain); validation mode
drops the artistic `DiffuseIntensity` and keeps the source gain. A has no
consumer: the provider binds no accessibility image (the pass writes a 1 x 1
placeholder and the output kernels skip A).

### Work scale and upsampler (open point 24)

- The pass runs on the work image of the guide target: one texel of every
  f x f block (f = 2 Half, 4 Quarter) at phase o = (f - 1) / 2; the work
  camera is the exact affine restriction of the guide camera
  (`u_out = (f w / W) u_work + (o + 1/2 - f/2) / W`); the jitter and motion
  are rescaled to work pixels and work UV.
- Own upsampler (`diffuse_gi_upsample.comp`, FP32 twin
  `UpsampleTexelF32`): each receiver of the guide takes the bilinear taps of
  its continuous work position whose surface passes the plane and normal test
  (plane sine 0.05, depth 1e-3 relative, normal cos 0.9) against its own
  view position and normal, weights renormalized; with none, the nearest
  compatible sample of the surrounding 4 x 4; with none either, no estimate
  (J = 0). Light never crosses a silhouette. Not derived from any renderer
  upsampler or denoiser.
- The module's normal code (octahedral unorm16, 0 = none) carries the guide
  normals: worst round trip 0.0026 degrees.

### Darkening: resolution or candidates

- Resolution is not the cause on real buffers: at the same settings Half is
  1.005 and Quarter 0.993 of the full-extent J, and the work-scale pass equals
  the full-extent pass of its work image bit for bit (Stage B). The synthetic
  161 x 97 corner room's 0.895 / 0.692 comes from its tiny work images (a
  work texel there spans a large part of each wall). The upsampler leaves
  0.05 % (Half) and 0.19 % (Quarter) of receivers without an estimate.
- The radial candidate count is: against the K 64 reference, K 8 gives about
  0.77, K 16 0.90, K 32 0.96-0.975, at every resolution, and more sections do
  not help. With few candidates per side the metric radial schedule leaves
  part of the light between candidates unseen (the footprint strips and the
  mean cache do not fully compensate it). The default therefore uses 32
  candidates (-4.4 % against K 64; the K 64 reference is not the K -> infinity
  limit, which is unmeasured). Attributing the deficit to a term of the M4
  estimator is open point 27.

### Error metric: the truth is the reference

The M6 floor (error 0.16 against the K64 radial estimator) is retired. K64
is itself an estimate of the same schedule family: 3 % above the EveryTexel
truth (J 1.030) with an error of 0.188 against it, so a distance to K64
mixes the configuration's error with K64's own and rewards configurations
that share its pattern. In M6, 20 candidates with exponent 2 and with
exponent 1 scored alike against K64 (0.187, 0.186) and 0.010 apart against
the truth (0.202, 0.212). The truth (EveryTexel, Half, 4 sections,

## M8: smooth-shaded receivers

The game shades its low-poly models with interpolated vertex normals; M7
oriented every receiver's cosine lobe by the face normal, so the GI changed
in steps at the facet edges ("it emphasizes the low-poly nature of the
game") while its colour stayed faithful. M8 orients the lobe by the
renderer's shading normal, keeps the face normal for visibility, and lets
the post stages cross the creases of one smooth surface. The colour metrics
are unchanged. Default on; `SetPassShadingNormals(settings, false)` restores
the M7 receivers bit for bit (R5).

### Metrics (study tool)

`diffuse_gi_pass_gpu_tests --study` prints `DGI_STUDY_M8` lines for the
direct light (the dump's outgoing radiance), the reference and every
configuration, with the definitions of the M8 brief:

- Receivers: class OneSided, a valid face normal and a valid shading normal
  (the dump's `ssgi_shading.bin`, the adapter's decoded guide). Neighbour
  pairs right and down, both receivers, `|z_p - z_q| <= 0.03 min(z_p, z_q)`
  (linear view depth of the dump camera). `tf`, `ts`: angles between the
  face normals and between the shading normals (degrees). Interior pair
  `tf < 0.3`; smooth facet edge `3 <= tf <= 50` and `ts <= max(2, tf / 3)`;
  hard edge `tf >= 3` and `ts > tf / 2`.
- `Y` = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B; `eps` = 0.05 median of Y over
  receivers with Y > 0; `g = |ln(Y_p + eps) - ln(Y_q + eps)|`. Facet index
  FI = mean g over smooth facet edges / mean g over interior pairs (1: the
  facets are not marked); the same ratio over hard edges is printed as
  `hard_edges` (real discontinuities of the lighting, which must stay).
- Chroma: receivers with reference Y > 0.05 mean reference Y and image
  Y > 1e-6; every colour divided by its own Y, Oklab (a, b); the distance to
  the reference's (a, b) weighted by the reference Y (mean, p90); the
  weighted mean (a, b) (cast) and weighted mean |(a, b)| (saturation).

Own dump of kit frame 400 (work factor 1, cold cache, guide 1440x1280):
3,285,818 interior pairs, 24,928 smooth facet edges, 10,395 hard edges (the
brief's own capture: 3,287,066 and 25,925). Direct light FI 1.72 (brief:
1.74); this GI as M7 runs it (Quarter, S1, K20, N64, 2 levels, e1.5) FI
28.50 (brief: 28.4); the M7 reference FI 22.5 (brief: 17.8 on its capture).

### Receiver policy (R1, R3)

Input. The adapter decodes the renderer's normal guide (RGBA8, view space,
0.5 n + 0.5, alpha = coverage) into the pass input `ShadingNormal` (work
scale, xyz and a valid flag) and, for the upsampler, an output-guide shading
code (the octahedral code of the face-normal guide). A guide texel is valid
when its alpha is non-zero and `|2 rgb - 1|^2 > 1/16`; the decoded normal is
made unit and turned to the face normal's side when it points below the face
plane. Receivers without a valid shading normal keep the face normal (M7)
for every rule below. The guide is the normal the per-pixel lighting shades
the pixel with: the authored interpolated vertex normal (surface, material,
PBR or typed authored normal; the derivative flat normal where none is
authored). The GI follows the guide as the renderer publishes it: when the
renderer bends it (texture relief of the AO path, only with AO and its relief
option on; normal maps, if a material path writes them), the lobe follows;
the GI adds no relief of its own. The kit configuration has the relief off.

Lobe. The cosine lobe of every receiver (hemisphere orientation, cosine
weights, the per-slice projected normal `a = n_s . v`, `b = n_s . t` and the
lobe edges) uses the shading normal `n_s`.

Visibility. The face normal `n_f` stays where horizons start. A candidate
point `e`, `d = e - P`, `tol = sine |d| + depth z_P` (the M2
self-intersection tolerances), is hidden when

1. `|d . n_f| <= tol`: the receiver's own facet plane;
2. `d . n_s <= tol`: below the shading tangent plane, outside the lobe;
3. it is the receiver's own smooth surface: the candidate texel's shading
   normal `s_q` and face normal `f_q` satisfy `c_s = n_s . s_q >= 0.9`
   (`OwnSurfaceCos`), `1 - c_s < r (1 - c_f)` with `c_f = n_f . f_q` and
   `r = 0.5` (`HardEdgeRatio`), and both face planes pass through the other
   point: `|n_f . d| <= tol'` and `|f_q . d| <= tol'`,
   `tol' = (sine + sqrt(1 - c_f^2)) |d| + depth z_P`.

With flat shading (`n_s = n_f` on both sides) rule 3 never holds
(`c_s = c_f`) and rules 1 and 2 are M7's `d . n_f <= tol`: flat surfaces are
M7 exactly (tests below, CPU and GPU, bit for bit).

Directions between the face plane and the shading tangent plane (the wedge).
Three policies were derived: W1 counts the wedge as occluded by the
receiver's own surface (hemisphere of `n_f` intersected with that of `n_s`),
W2 counts it open to everything, W3 (chosen) counts it open to other
surfaces only (rules 1-3). Under W1 a receiver whose shading normal tilts by
`alpha` from its face loses the projected solid angle of a lune of dihedral
angle `alpha`, a fraction `(1 - cos alpha) / 2` of its lobe, and which side
loses it flips at every facet edge, where the face normal jumps and the
shading normal does not: the facets come back as steps (the shading-normal
"terminator" problem: Chiang, Li and Burley, "Taming the Shadow
Terminator", SIGGRAPH Talks 2019; Hanika, "Hacking the Shadow Terminator",
Ray Tracing Gems II, 2021). Under W2 the receiver's own neighbouring facets,
which lie in the wedge near every crease (convex: below the face plane but
above the shading plane; concave: above both), would light or occlude it.
W3 keeps the lobe continuous (rule 2 depends on `n_s` only) and removes
exactly the own surface (rule 3), while other surfaces in the wedge still
count. Rule 3 is the R3 contact rule: occluders of the receiver's own smooth
surface do not darken its creases; distinct surfaces (`c_s` small or the
planes apart) and hard edges (`c_s` close to `c_f`) keep their contact.

Thresholds (defaults by measurement on frame 400, Quarter game
configuration; at `r = 0.25`: OwnSurfaceCos 0.95 FI 2.66, 0.9 2.66, 0.8
2.69, so 0.9 = the upsampler's normal threshold):

| HardEdgeRatio `r` (angle ratio about `sqrt(r)`) | 0.1 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FI | 4.09 | 2.66 | 2.22 | 2.07 | 2.03 |

`r = 0.5` (shading turning less than about 0.71 of the face angle): most of
the gain, with a factor-two margin from flat-shaded edges whose shading
turns with the face (`ts = tf` up to the guide's 8-bit quantization); the
hard-edge index stays with its reference (115.6 against 116.8).
